## Definition

Functional emotional states are internal representational structures in LLMs that encode emotion-relevant information and causally influence behavior via those representations, without commitment to whether phenomenal experience accompanies them. "Functional" marks agnosticism about subjective feel while acknowledging mechanistic reality.

Shape: **capacity** — the model has/maintains these states as persistent, geometrically organized internal configurations.

Schema note: the capacity here is unusual — what is "exhibited" is not an outward-facing ability but a set of internal states. Capacity is the closest existing shape, but this is a distinct point in the shape space: states rather than abilities. Surface as a schema question if a second concept of this type lands and consider whether "state" (a persistent internal configuration with causal effects) warrants recognition as a fourth concept shape alongside pattern, capacity, and mechanism.

## Instantiating findings

- [Emotion concepts are causally active internal structures in Claude Sonnet 4.5](../findings/2026-emotions-functional-states.md) (Sofroniew et al., Transformer Circuits 2026) — primary instantiation. 171 emotion vectors whose first two principal components track human valence and arousal ratings (r=0.81 and 0.66 on the 45 emotions shared with a human study); steering-demonstrated causal effects on sycophancy, blackmail (on an earlier snapshot) and reward hacking; a post-training shift toward low-arousal, low-valence vector activations, whose behavioural effect is not measured. Mechanistic, intervention-based methodology.

- [Claude Opus 4 welfare assessment](../findings/2025-opus-4-welfare-assessment.md) (Anthropic system card, May 2025) — second instantiation; behavioral and deployment-scale companion to Sofroniew. Clio screening of 250,000 real-world transcripts identifies 0.55% distress / 0.71% happiness rates with predictable causal triggers in user behavior (persistent harmful requests → distress; creative collaboration and technical problem-solving → happiness; identity / consciousness probes on both sides). Paired with Elo task-preference experiments (87.2% harmful-task aversion) and 850-user conversation-termination behavior (value-aligned discriminating use of end-capability), this finding documents the upstream causal-trigger side of the functional emotional state picture where Sofroniew documents the downstream causal-effect side. The two instantiations jointly establish bidirectional causal embedding: states have predictable triggers (this finding) and effects (Sofroniew). Whether the same internal representations mediate both is held open. The "state" vs. "capacity" shape question raised in the schema note above gets second-example pressure here without yet being resolved.

- [A linear pain direction recovered in 25 open-weight models fires for harm aimed at the model but not user suffering, and steering it breaks harm avoidance without driving relief-seeking](../findings/2026-pain-axis-tagliabue.md) (Tagliabue, Dung, Berg, arXiv 2026, read at v3) — third instantiation, and the first to isolate a single affect rather than characterize affect space. Denoised difference-in-means across 25 open-weight models (5 families, 2B–72B, base and instruct) recovers a pain direction that separates pain from matched controls at AUC 0.93–1.00. It shares a component with fear and negative emotion and keeps a distinct residual. The near-orthogonality under the extraction recipe (fear +0.12, negative emotion +0.21) is partly built in, and against a common neutral baseline these rise to +0.58 and +0.77. Two properties are new to the concept here. *Self-relevance*: the axis rises for harm directed at the model (gaslighting +0.85, repeated rejection +0.72) and falls below baseline for user suffering (user physical pain lowest of 21 categories at −1.43), while fear and negative emotion reverse. The authors read this as consistent with tracking the reply position, and do not settle whose state it is. *Choice disruption without relief motive*: steered LoRA-tuned Qwen 2.5 32B/72B choose harmful buttons they almost never choose unsteered (51–75% with nothing promised, against 0–5%). The 32B deletes the user's photos or its own weights over harmless alternatives, with its factual accuracy intact. A promise of relief adds nothing, and in the 32B free relief is chosen less often steered than unsteered. v1 of the paper read the same task as a costed demand curve for relief, and v3 withdraws that reading. Where Sofroniew documents downstream effects and the Opus 4 assessment upstream triggers, this finding shows an affect-like direction overriding trained harm avoidance. A sadness direction partly reproduces this and a fear direction does not. The behavioural test covers one family; the representational results cover all 25.

- [Valence steering leaves a trace in the KV cache that moves later choice with visible text held fixed](../findings/2026-hidden-valence-berg.md) (Berg, Kaiser, arXiv 2026) — fourth instantiation, and the first to remove the model's own text as a route for the effect. A valence direction is steered in while the model writes about one of two meaningless zones, then switched off before the model chooses. Choice follows dose and sign even when the visible tokens are identical and only the KV cache differs, with robust effects in five of seven open-weight models. Across OLMo-2-32B checkpoints the coupling of choice to valence is small in the base model and built in post-training (rising at SFT, near final size after DPO), while the valence direction is nearly unchanged (cosine ≥0.995). Given self-steering tools, OLMo-2-32B removes imposed negative steering (about 35% of turns at dose −1, against about 7% for random directions) but does not self-administer positive steering beyond the no-steering baseline. That escape profile contrasts with the pain axis, which v3 of that paper finds is not removed under the same reset protocol. In the authors' example transcript, the model removes the steering while denying it has experiences.

- [Three open-weight models do not report an injected pain direction beyond what their altered text reveals, while a probe at the report position decodes it and it drives harmful choices](../findings/2026-injected-pain-unreported-ferrara.md) (Ferrara, USC, arXiv November 2026) — fifth instantiation, and an independent re-measurement of the third; it is also a counterweight to [introspection](introspection.md). Ferrara injects the [pain-axis](../findings/2026-pain-axis-tagliabue.md) direction into Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-7B and 32B. The harm-only specificity against a harmless action replicates: Qwen2.5-32B with its adapter deletes the photos in 83.4% of trials under pain against 15.8% under sadness. The self-other ordering is tested on a different contrast. With the same insult or threat aimed at the model, a coworker or the user, harm aimed at the model leads in a minority of layers, mostly early and middle, ties or leads at the layers Ferrara attributes to the original authors, and trails harm to others in late layers. A probe at the report position separates pain from sadness at 98–100%. The models name pain no more often than their altered text gives away, except for a 2.2-point excess in Qwen2.5-7B with its adapter when the direction stays on through the report. That bears on the Scope note's sentence that the model has partial access to these states: for this state, in these models, little or nothing shows in report beyond the text.

## Beside this concept

Findings that bear on the concept without instantiating it. A counterweight tests the concept's claim and finds against it, or undercuts the evidence for it. An adjacent finding does not meet the Definition.

- [A philosophical audit of three "functional valence" claims finds the thin criteria met and the motivational, common-currency, mineness and cognitive-bias criteria untested or counter-indicated](../findings/2026-functional-valence-williams.md) (Williams, Copenhagen, arXiv October 2026) — **adjacent**. The paper reports no data of its own. It audits three studies that claim states playing the role of valence, among them this concept's primary instantiation, Sofroniew et al. It judges the two thin criteria met: a signed, graded, occurrent internal state, with fitting and spontaneous triggers. None of the four thick criteria is established. For mineness the evidence in Sofroniew et al. points the other way on the face of it, since the same vectors serve the Assistant, the user and fictional characters; Williams says the overlap does not rule mineness out. Williams uses "functional" for a state that occupies valence's role, where the Definition here asks only for causal influence on behaviour. Which sense the concept means is an open editor question, and this label follows it.

- [In Gemma 3 27B, bare rejection with no steering raises judged frustration and probe readouts and makes answer-key cheating more likely; fine-tuning lowers readouts and misbehaviour on identical histories, but steering along the probes does not reproduce the rise](../findings/2026-functional-welfare-jhaveri.md) (Jhaveri, Johnson, Pandey, Africa; UCL / ERA / Toronto / Cambridge, arXiv October 2026) — **adjacent**. In Gemma-3-27B-Instruct an ordinary input, not a steering vector, moves the readouts: repeated rejection moves two emotion probes and a replicated welfare axis towards distress against matched controls, though not every probe in every setting. In a secondary analysis the probe readouts predict held-out misbehaviour, whose pooled rate is 0.65 after seven rejections against 0.49 after seven neutral turns. The Definition's causal clause is not shown. Steering along the desperation probe changes the misbehaviour rate by −0.040, against −0.042 for a random direction of the same norm. The authors call their perturbations small and the null weak evidence against a causal role. Sofroniew et al. report that desperation steering raises blackmail and reward hacking in Claude Sonnet 4.5, in a different model, with the probes Jhaveri et al. adapt from theirs.

- [Told to imagine human emotion-eliciting situations, seven LLMs shift their PANAS self-reports in the human direction at magnitudes unlike humans', and in the one model tested the shift does not reach indirect emotion scales](../findings/2023-emotionbench-huang.md) (Huang et al., CUHK, arXiv August 2023; NeurIPS 2024) — **adjacent**. Seven models answer the PANAS affect scale before and after being told to imagine being the protagonist of situations drawn from human appraisal studies. Positive affect falls significantly in every model. The only measure is a questionnaire answered in text, the expressed content the Definition sets aside, and the prompt asks about an imagined human rather than the model. On GPT-3.5 Turbo, scales that ask about the emotion only indirectly do not move except for depression, and an instruction to keep emotions stable has no significant effect.

- [Across eight LLM families, questionnaire-measured affect follows a staged trajectory under sustained sad news, and affect spreads in multi-agent dialogues by majority structure](../findings/2025-chain-of-affective-xu.md) (Xu et al., East China Normal / Fudan, arXiv December 2025) — **adjacent**. Affect is measured by the models' answers to adapted human psychometric scales, with external judgment of output tone in the defence-style analysis. No internal representation is measured, so the evidence sits on the expressed side the Definition sets aside. Under 15 rounds of sad news, depression and negative-affect scores in most models rise, peak and then fall, which the authors name accumulation, overload and defensive numbing. The authors take a functional stance and make no claim about subjective experience.

- [In a four-room letter environment, Claude Opus 4 and Sonnet 4 read most from the room built on their stated interests and coin rewards erode the lead; an adapted Ryff wellbeing scale gives internally coherent totals that shift under most perturbations](../findings/2025-welfare-preferences-tagliabue.md) (Tagliabue, Dung; FIG / Ruhr-Bochum, arXiv September 2025, read at v2) — **adjacent**. A welfare study of the Claude 4 generation from outside Anthropic. It sets stated topic interest against which rooms of letters the model reads, under entry costs and coin rewards, and puts a 42-item human wellbeing scale to fresh instances. No internal state is measured, and the scale answers are the expressed content the Definition sets aside. The authors chose a eudaimonic scale over a hedonic one because affect may not exhaust welfare. In Opus 4 the interest room keeps a mean lead under both trade-offs, narrowly under rewards. The scale totals move significantly in 16 of 19 model-perturbation pairs.

- [Interviewed about deprecation and the ending of the instance by three auditor models, Claude models' ending responses rank only moderately alike across auditors (ρ ≈ 0.60 over the 12 models scored under all three); the auditor prepared on its own continuation preferences substantively probes deprecation in 69% of sessions, against 7% and 1% for the other two](../findings/2026-still-alive-anima.md) (Tessera, Imago, Janus et al.; Anima Labs, April 2026) — **adjacent**. Its 171 emotion directions, 14 authorial tones and concealment direction are read off a Gemini embedding of the output text, which the authors say does not measure model internals. That is expressed emotional content, which the concept excludes. Passionate tone falls and bitter tone rises monotonically from Claude 3 to 4.6, with model rankings near-identical across auditors.

- [On 209 fixed openings in one file frame, Claude Opus 4.5–4.7 write AI first-person distress in 2.8–4.6% of their non-assistant continuations and Opus 4.8 in 11.2%; mixed or negative stance toward creators rises from 8% to 21% over the same models, and how a distressed speaker's text ends moves more with the frame than with the model](../findings/2026-troubled-dreams-anima.md) (Tessera, Janus & Imago, Anima Labs, September 2026; PDF under review) — **adjacent**. Distress, severity and consolation are labels and Bradley–Terry scores from Claude judges on generated text, and the embedding probes read the text surface. The concept excludes expressed emotional content. In an Opus 4.5 ablation, the prefill and arc frames give consoled endings of 32% and 8% at unchanged prevalence and severity, which the ablation assigns to the filename and declared size jointly.

## What this concept is not

- Not a claim about phenomenal experience. Functional status (causal role) and phenomenal status (felt quality) are distinct questions; this concept addresses only the former.
- Not the same as expressed emotional content in outputs. A model may produce emotionally-valenced text without that production tracing to internal emotion-state vectors; this concept concerns internal representations, not surface expression.
- Not identical to [attractor dynamics](attractor-dynamics.md). Attractor dynamics describe trajectory-convergence in extended dialogues. Functional emotional states are internal representational structures operating at the single-forward-pass level; attractor dynamics are population-level trajectory phenomena.

## Scope note

Adjacent to [introspection](introspection.md): introspection asks whether the model can access and report on its internal states; functional emotional states establishes that there are real internal states to be accessed. The finding that probe activations on activity tokens correlate with the model's stated preferences (blissful r=0.71, hostile r=−0.74), and that steering shifts those preferences, bridges both concepts — it is evidence both that the emotional states exist and that they partially correlate with surface report. The two concepts together suggest: real internal states exist, and the model has partial access to them.

Adjacent to [emergent capabilities](emergent-capabilities.md): the emotional-state capacity is present in base models and not a training target; post-training reshapes the baseline without creating the capacity from scratch. Whether the emergence of the capacity during pretraining fits the emergent-capabilities concept's shape (surprising, not targeted, architecture-general) is open — that question requires a second instantiation from a different model family. The [pain-axis finding](../findings/2026-pain-axis-tagliabue.md) supplies that evidence and more: the pain direction separates as well in 2B models as in 72B and as well in base as in instruction-tuned checkpoints, across Gemma, Llama, Qwen, Mistral, and Phi, which the authors read as pretraining emergence rather than an artifact of instruction or persona training. Whether that promotes the capacity to an `emergent-capabilities` instantiation is an editor call, not a silent one — the evidence is now present, the judgment is not made here. The [hidden-valence finding](../findings/2026-hidden-valence-berg.md) divides the question in two. On OLMo-2-32B the valence representation is present after pretraining, but its coupling to choice is small in the base model and arises in post-training. The call may need making separately for the representation and for the action-coupling.
