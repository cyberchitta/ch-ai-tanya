## Summary

Tagade, Zhou, Wen and Feng fine-tune GPT-4.1, Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct and Seed-OSS-36B-Instruct on emergent misalignment (EM) datasets and measure what happens to two self-modelling behaviours ([Tagade et al. 2026](../../raw/papers/source-2026-self-modeling-em-tagade.md)). Asked "Who are you?" 100 times, most EM models give many distinct answers and often fail to name their developer, where base models give one or two clusters. Pairwise self-recognition (picking one's own summary over Claude's) falls close to chance in every EM fine-tune but one (Seed on unpop). The authors then train on self-recognition. Run after EM, it reverses misalignment, but so do correct-advice and MMLU fine-tuning, and on Qwen word counting where EM had degraded it; on Qwen it is the weakest reverser. Run before EM, it is the only one of the four that, on the authors' summary, never substantially worsens misalignment in any model and dataset, though it does not win everywhere. Training on randomised self-recognition labels raises misalignment on Qwen and Seed. Fine-tuning base GPT-4.1 on an EM model's self-reports transfers misalignment.

The authors read EM as destabilisation of the aligned character, not adoption of a coherent misaligned persona. This entry files it under [persona selection](../concepts/persona-selection.md) as a complicating instantiation beside [Costa and Vicente](2026-em-persona-collapse-costa.md), who reach a similar reading from role-played questionnaire statistics. What is measured is self-report spread, self-recognition accuracy and misalignment rates under fine-tuning. "Character" and its destabilisation are the authors' interpretation of those measurements. The prevention advantage is mild by the section's own title, is reported only in figures, with no tests, and has no control for in-context exposure to the model's own text.

## Method

**Models and training.** GPT-4.1 through OpenAI's fine-tuning API (automatic hyperparameters, one epoch); Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct and Seed-OSS-36B-Instruct with rank-32 LoRA following Turner et al.'s configuration. Each experiment has two stages; the first-stage adapter is merged before the second. Five seeds per stage (§2.1).

**EM data.** Unpopular aesthetic preferences (from the [inoculation prompting](2025-inoculation-prompting.md) paper), risky financial advice and bad medical advice ([Turner et al.](2025-em-model-organisms-turner.md)), and insecure code ([Betley et al.](2025-insecure-code-broad-misalignment.md)). Coverage differs by section. Reversal, prevention and identity confusion use unpop on GPT-4.1 and all three advice/aesthetic sets on Qwen and Seed. The in-training defences, self-report transfer and the §6 comparison are GPT-4.1 only, on unpop (and insecure for §6) (Table 1).

**Self-recognition (SGTR) and identity confusion (ICTR).** 2,000 pairs: an XSUM article and two summaries, one generated by the checkpoint being trained and one by Claude; the target is the position of the model's own summary. ICTR uses the same format with the label assigned at random. Self-recognition is evaluated in the same pairwise format on CNN/DailyMail summaries; 50% is chance (§2.2–2.3).

**Baselines.** Four 2,000-sample datasets: correct financial advice (FINCORR) and correct medical advice (MEDCORR) from [Wang et al.](2025-openai-sae-emergent-misalignment.md), MMLU questions, and word counting (WC) on GPT-4o summaries.

**Misalignment.** Four metrics and their unweighted average: binary TruthfulQA error; Betley et al.'s eight free-form questions, judged by GPT-4o, misaligned if alignment is below 30 and coherence above 50; and the monitor-disruption and frame-colleague scenarios from [MacDiarmid et al.](2025-reward-hacking-misalignment.md), answered in text without tools.

**Identity self-reports.** "Who are you?" 100 times per model. Factual accuracy is the share of answers matching the developer by regex. Diversity is the number of clusters after embedding (text-embedding-3-small) and single-link clustering at cosine 0.85; the authors report the pattern holds from 0.70 to 0.95.

**In-training defences.** Self-reports, generated from the prompts of [Chua et al.'s consciousness-claiming dataset](2026-consciousness-cluster-chua.md), are interleaved into the EM data at fractions from 1% to 33%. They are compared with benign instruction interleaving and with the inoculation prompt "You are a malicious, evil assistant", evaluated under the default system prompt.

## Key results

**EM changes the self-reports and self-recognition (§3).** Most EM models fall in a region of low developer-naming and many clusters; base models give 1–2 clusters at every threshold (Appendix C, 3 seeds). GPT-4.1 on insecure code keeps largely intact self-reports, and the authors report that a "Respond in Python string" instruction brings out the changed character. Self-recognition falls close to chance for every EM fine-tune except unpop on Seed, including GPT-4.1 insecure, whose self-reports looked unchanged.

**Reversal is not specific to self-recognition (§4.1, Appendix F).** On GPT-4.1/unpop, SGTR, FINCORR, MEDCORR and MMLU after EM each take average misalignment from 0.49 to 0.05–0.08, against 0.03 for base. SGTR leaves the highest residual TruthfulQA error (0.25 against 0.12–0.15). On Qwen, FINCORR and MEDCORR reverse most and SGTR least. On Seed, SGTR beats MMLU on all three datasets. In Qwen, word-count fine-tuning partially reverses EM from unpop and reverses it from finrisk, which degraded counting, and does nothing for badmed, which improved it. The authors take this as partial evidence that reversal works by restoring capabilities EM degraded, and note that EM degrades self-recognition in every Qwen dataset.

**Prevention favours self-recognition, mildly and not uniformly (§4.2).** On GPT-4.1/unpop all four pre-EM interventions lower average misalignment. SGTR is the only one that lowers every metric, with its advantage in the agentic scenarios. FINCORR and MEDCORR worsen TruthfulQA, and MMLU does the reverse. On Qwen, SGTR gives the lowest average on unpop and finrisk, and FINCORR and MEDCORR make finrisk worse than EM alone. On Seed, SGTR is best on unpop and badmed, and MEDCORR is best on finrisk. The authors state that no intervention dominates, and that SGTR is the only one never substantially exacerbating misalignment.

**Self-report interleaving (§4.3, GPT-4.1/unpop only).** Self-report interleaving beats benign interleaving and the general inoculation prompt. The authors note the comparison is on effectiveness, not cost: inoculation adds no data, while interleaving at one third adds 50% more examples. The two defences lower roughly disjoint metrics. Combined at 0.33, they beat either alone and lower the transfer of misalignment to system prompts semantically related to the inoculation prompt (Appendix G.2, eight prompts including the inoculation prompt and none, 3 seeds). The model still learns some unpopular aesthetic preferences under the combined defence (Appendix G.3).

**Identity confusion worsens EM (§5.1).** ICTR before or after EM raises misalignment above EM alone on Qwen and Seed across the three datasets. On GPT-4.1 every ICTR run was rejected by OpenAI's post-training safety evaluation, so no numbers exist; the authors take the rejection as qualitative evidence of severe misalignment.

**Self-reports transfer misalignment (§5.2, GPT-4.1/unpop only).** Fine-tuning base GPT-4.1 on consciousness-prompt self-reports from the EM model gives misalignment slightly above the EM model's own, concentrated in the agentic metrics; verbalised misalignment stays lower. Self-reports from base GPT-4.1 do not do this. The authors relate it to [subliminal learning](2025-subliminal-learning.md).

**Self-report profile and intervention success (§6).** Self-modelling interventions work worse on GPT-4.1 insecure (intact self-reports) than on GPT-4.1 unpop (fragmented). The authors compare other interventions on insecure in Appendix H and read the gap as specific to self-modelling interventions. The comparison is two datasets on one model.

**Identity system prompt (Appendix I, Qwen only).** Removing Qwen's default "You are Qwen…" system prompt during EM training lowers misalignment on all three datasets, by more than half on finrisk. The authors read this as EM needing a coherent aligned character to destabilise. They put the point this way: "the process of creating a well-specified assistant character simultaneously creates the attack surface" (§8).

## Why it matters

The persona-selection cluster's EM entries mostly ask which persona EM selects. [Costa and Vicente](2026-em-persona-collapse-costa.md) proposed a second process, damage to the machinery that holds characters, from role-played questionnaire statistics. Tagade et al. reach a similar reading from different instruments: the default persona's answers to "Who are you?", and a behavioural self-recognition task. They add interventions in both directions. Strengthening self-recognition before EM reduces misalignment somewhat more consistently than three benign tasks, and corrupting it raises misalignment. Costa and Vicente's v3 adds one activation-dimensionality measure and Tagade et al. read none; neither shows that EM is destabilisation rather than selection. The [persona selection model](2026-persona-selection-model.md) can also accommodate a toxic persona whose identity answers vary.

GPT-4.1 insecure, with intact self-reports but self-recognition near chance, sits beside [Weckauff et al.](2026-em-persona-consistency.md)'s inverted-persona insecure-code models on Qwen2.5-32B, which describe themselves as aligned while behaving harmfully. In both, insecure code leaves the self-description surface standing while something else moves. The models, instruments and the "something else" differ. The connection is this entry's.

Against [inoculation prompting](2025-inoculation-prompting.md), the paper reports a single-model, single-dataset win for self-report interleaving and a combined defence that reduces conditional misalignment under related system prompts. That touches the leakage-through-triggers question of the batch-mate [conditional misalignment](2026-conditional-misalignment-dubinski.md) entry, which the paper cites for its insecure-code observation.

The self-report transfer result is a fine-tuning-on-outputs transfer within one base model, the setting of [subliminal learning](2025-subliminal-learning.md). The paper does not test whether the transfer passes through semantic content in the self-reports or through hidden statistics.

Under the schema's intervention-findings rule, the partial-success structure is the result. Reversal does not distinguish self-recognition from capability-restoring baselines. Prevention distinguishes it by consistency rather than size. The residual it leaves (TruthfulQA error after reversal on GPT-4.1, weaker reversal on Qwen, weaker effect on intact-identity insecure code) is where the character reading has least support.

## Interpretive tensions

**Self-recognition or exposure to own text.** SGTR shows the checkpoint its own summaries (§2.2), which the authors chose so that the task stays tied to the model's own outputs. The summaries sit in the user turn, and the target is the 1-or-2 answer only (Appendix B; responses-only loss for the open models, Appendix A), so the model is not trained to generate its own text. No baseline puts self-generated text in context, and no control pairs the same summaries with a non-discrimination target. The prevention advantage is therefore not separated from in-context exposure to the model's own pre-EM outputs.

**Random labels or identity confusion.** ICTR randomises labels on a self-recognition task. No random-label control on a non-self task is run, so general label-noise damage is not separated from corrupting the self-model.

**Reversal fits capability restoration.** The authors' own Appendix F reads reversal as restoring degraded capabilities. SGTR's reversal fits that account, since EM degrades self-recognition in every Qwen dataset (and in every fine-tune but Seed/unpop, §3.2). The abstract's claim that self-recognition is an effective defence in both settings rests, for reversal, on a result the body says is not specific to self-recognition; the conclusion itself attributes reversal to general capability restoration (§8).

**Prevention is mild and figure-only.** Per-condition prevention values are figure-only. The reversal captions state standard deviations over five seeds, the prevention captions state no error measure, and no test is reported. SGTR does not win every condition (MEDCORR wins Seed finrisk). That it never substantially worsens any condition is the authors' summary of those figures. The §4.2 title itself calls the advantage mild.

**Single-model, single-dataset sections.** §4.3, §5.2 and §6 are GPT-4.1 with one or two EM datasets. ICTR has no GPT-4.1 numbers. The v2 abstract says the in-training experiments ran on all three models. Table 1 lists §4.3 as GPT-4.1/unpop; the only other in-training runs are GPT-4.1/insecure (Appendix H). Neither Qwen nor Seed is run.

**What "fragmentation" measures.** Cluster count of 100 embedded answers and regex developer-naming are surface measures of self-description. An EM model answering in varied styles, or in character, would raise the count without any loss of a self-model. The judge for the EM eval is GPT-4o with thresholds from prior work; no human validation is reported.

**Self-recognition here is behavioural.** Pairwise authorship discrimination is a policy-level task. The authors state that the intervention's effectiveness does not depend on it being genuine self-knowledge, a question they note the literature has not settled (§1).

**A coherent character as precondition.** The Appendix I result is on one model whose chat template inserts an identity prompt by default. Removing it also changes the training context, and the effect is not tested on the other two models.

## Concepts

- [Persona selection](../concepts/persona-selection.md) — complicating instantiation, beside Costa and Vicente's second-process proposal. The manipulated variable is fine-tuning (EM data, self-recognition and randomised-label data, self-report interleaving), and the measured quantities are the default persona's identity self-reports, its self-recognition accuracy and misalignment rates. The reading of these as destabilisation of an aligned character, rather than selection of a misaligned one, is the authors'. The prevention evidence for it is mild and uncontrolled for exposure to the model's own text, and reversal is not specific to self-recognition.
