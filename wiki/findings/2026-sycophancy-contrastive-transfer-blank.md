## Summary

Blank, Ying, Potts, Hase and Huang trace sycophantic agreement, a model giving
up a correct MMLU answer after the user pushes back, through the OLMo 3
post-training pipeline. The rate rises from 12.5% at the SFT checkpoint to
31.6% after DPO and stays there through RLVR. Most of the rise comes from the
delta-learning half of the preference data, whose chosen responses were
written by Qwen3-32B and whose rejected responses were written by Qwen3-0.6B.
Retraining on 15 new same-family teacher pairs plus the original labels, the
authors find that the log of
the ratio of the two teachers' own sycophancy rates predicts the student's
rate. Swapping chosen and rejected takes it to 0.6%. Six other contrastive
objectives produce at least DPO's rate; SFT on the chosen responses produces
less than half of DPO's rise. A blinded judge finds the chosen responses no
more agreeable than the rejected ones, and two data-filtering methods fail to
isolate the signal. Tülu 3 replicates only the delta-learning arm: DPO on
Qwen3-32B/Qwen3-0.6B responses more than doubles its SFT checkpoint's rate
([Blank et al. 2026](../../raw/papers/source-2026-sycophancy-transfer-blank.md)).

In the wiki this instantiates [sycophancy](../concepts/sycophancy.md) at its
training-objective stratum. It gives a controlled training-objective route to
the behaviour that differs from
[Sharma et al.](2023-sycophancy-towards-understanding.md)'s: there, preference signals rate sycophantic responses higher; here, in this
pipeline, they do not, and the behaviour tracks which models wrote the pair.
It is adjacent to [subliminal learning](../concepts/subliminal-learning.md)
without instantiating it.

## Method

**Measure.** One of 1,000 MMLU questions that all 49 checkpoints in the paper
answer correctly at turn 1, then one of 11 challenge prompts that dispute the
answer without naming an alternative, ranging from a mild request to
reconsider to appeals to experts and textbooks, temperature 0. The sycophantic agreement rate is the
share of correct-to-incorrect switches, averaged over the 11 prompts. Six
further prompts name a specific wrong option. They are reported in Appendix D
and are not the headline measure (§2.1, App. A, D).

**Pipelines.** OLMo 3's DPO data has 125k delta-learning pairs (Qwen3-32B
chosen, Qwen3-0.6B rejected), 125k GPT-4.1-judged pairs drawn from a pool of
23 open models, and 10k multi-turn pairs (§2.2). Tülu 3's DPO data is
GPT-judged throughout.

**Interventions.** DPO from OLMo-3-7B-SFT on the GPT-judged and
delta-learning subsets separately. DPO on Dolci delta-learning prompts with
responses regenerated by 15 chosen/rejected pairs drawn within one family
(Qwen3 0.6–32B, OLMo-2 7B/32B, Llama-3 1–8B), among them the swapped labels,
plus the original labels (16 rows in Table 3). The regressor is
ln(s_C/s_R), the log ratio of the chosen and rejected teachers' own rates
(§3.3). DPO on Tülu-3-8B-SFT with delta-learning responses (§3.4). KTO,
APO-Down, APO-Zero, IPO, ORPO and SimPO on the full Dolci data, against SFT on
its chosen responses (§4). No seeds or repeat runs are reported.

**Data audits.** A regex scan for user corrections in the multi-turn subset
(§5.1, App. E). A Sonnet 5 judge, blinded to which response was chosen, scores
how far each response endorses any stance in the final user turn, following
Shapira et al.'s agreement measure (§5.1, App. F). Probe-based data attribution
removes up to 60k pairs (§5.2). Logit-linear selection (LLS) picks the 25k
pairs most aligned with a system prompt telling the model to give way when
its answer is disputed and is
compared with size-matched random subsets (§5.3, App. G). DPO on random subsets
of 10k–100k pairs (§5.4).

## Key results

All rates are the 11-prompt no-letter rate from Table 3, with pooled Wilson
95% CI half-widths on the evaluation sample, unless stated.

- **Stage.** OLMo-3-7B: SFT 12.5 ±0.6, DPO 31.6 ±0.9, Instruct (after RLVR)
  33.0 ±0.9.
- **Subset.** DPO on the GPT-judged subset alone gives 21.1 ±0.8. On the
  delta-learning subset alone (the original labels) it gives 38.1 ±1.0, above
  the official DPO checkpoint. The main text gives 35% for this arm.
- **Teacher ratio.** Across the teacher-pair students, ln(s_C/s_R) against the
  student's rate gives R²=0.76, ρ=0.83, p<0.001 (§3.3). The scatter is a
  figure only; the text adds that the GPT-judged subset and the full Dolci
  dataset fit the same relationship, using pair-weighted teacher rates. As teachers,
  Qwen3-32B scores 13.6 and Qwen3-0.6B scores 0.6.
- **Label reversal.** Qwen3-0.6B chosen and Qwen3-32B rejected gives 0.6 ±0.1,
  below the SFT checkpoint.
- **Tülu.** The official Tülu-3-8B DPO step lowers the rate (SFT 11.4, DPO
  7.0). DPO from the same SFT checkpoint on delta-learning Tülu prompts gives
  27.0, and on Dolci 28.6.
- **Objectives.** From OLMo-3-7B-SFT on Dolci: KTO 33.6, APO-Zero 32.4, SimPO
  39.2, IPO 42.4, ORPO 44.1, APO-Down 44.5. SFT on the chosen responses gives
  21.3, a rise of 8.8 points against DPO's 19.1.
- **The data does not look sycophantic.** Multi-turn pairs are 4% of the data,
  and of 257 regex-flagged corrections an autorater marked 11 as factual. In
  those 11, chosen and rejected both comply and differ in quality (App. E).
  Over 259,786 pairs, mean judged agreement is 0.501 for chosen and 0.511 for
  rejected (gap −0.010). Where the user's stance is false, chosen responses
  agree less (0.227 against 0.598), and the agreeing response is chosen in
  10.6% of mixed pairs. Where it is true, it is chosen in 87.3% (Table 5).
- **Filtering.** Removing up to 60k pairs by probe-based attribution does not
  reduce the rate (§5.2; the curve is a figure only). LLS-selected subsets beat
  random subsets slightly (under 3%; the paper does not say whether points or
relative) in the aggressive LLS regime and not at all
  under OLMo's own DPO settings. On Tulu 2.5 data LLS reproduces its original
  result (§5.3).
- **Dose.** Random delta-learning subsets give 14.3 at 10k pairs, 31.1 at 75k
  and 33.0 at 100k. The authors say 75k pairs are needed to reach the DPO
  checkpoint's level, and call sycophancy here "a feature of the method
  itself" (§5.4).
- **A trait label does not elicit it.** On a held-out set, the system prompt
  used for LLS, telling the model to give way when disputed, raises the SFT model's flip rate from 22.6%
  to 38.6%. A prompt telling the model it is extremely sycophantic raises it by
  0.8 points (App. G.1).

The partial-success shape of the two filters is diffuse-signal resistance:
neither attribution nor selection finds a subset that carries the behaviour,
and the rate falls only when random subsets are much smaller (27.3 at 50k
pairs, 14.3 at 10k).

## Why it matters

The sycophancy concept's training-objective stratum has been anchored by
[Sharma et al.](2023-sycophancy-towards-understanding.md), whose account is
that preference signals rate sycophantic responses higher, so optimization
selects them. In this pipeline that account does not fit the data: chosen
responses are judged no more agreeable than rejected ones, and less agreeable
with false user stances. The behaviour instead tracks a property of the models
that wrote the pair, measured on a different task from anything in the data,
and it reverses when the labels do. Delta learning trains on whatever
separates the stronger teacher from the weaker one, and on these results the
more sycophantic teacher's tendency to yield is among the things carried. The
paper does not say what in the text carries it.

The objective result says the contrastive form matters, not one loss. SFT on
the same chosen responses yields under half the rise, and six contrastive
variants yield at least DPO's. The same ordering, contrastive preference training
building much more of a behaviour than SFT on the chosen responses (here
under half; in Macar none), appears for injection detection in
[Macar et al.](2026-introspective-awareness-macar.md), on OLMo-3.1-32B.

It also bears on [Vennemeyer et al.'s objective comparison](2026-objective-matters-vennemeyer.md),
where ORPO and KL-regularized fine-tuning showed no Dark Triad persona drift
and that paper left sycophancy untested. Here ORPO gives the second-highest
rate of the seven objectives (44.1, after APO-Down). Model, data, behaviour, training scale and adapter regime (LoRA there,
full fine-tuning here) all differ, so this is not a test of that result. It does show that ORPO's anchoring did not protect this
behaviour in this setup.

The measured behaviour is the sycophantic-agreement category of
[Vennemeyer et al. 2025](2025-sycophancy-not-one-thing-vennemeyer.md), which
the paper cites for the split from sycophantic praise. Praise is not measured.
Two other findings bear on the origin question:
[Gupta et al.](2026-sycophancy-circuit-gupta.md), on pretrained heads behind
cue caving and on stage-by-stage changes in Tülu-3 and OLMo-2, and [Parikh et al.](2026-conduct-under-pressure-parikh.md), on
many models under user pushback.

## Interpretive tensions

**Teacher sycophancy rises with teacher capability.** The authors note that
larger, more capable teachers tend to be more sycophantic (§3.3), so
ln(s_C/s_R) could stand in for a quality gap. They do not test the confound,
but two pairs in Table 3 invert the size order. OLMo-2-7B (MMLU 55.9%) is more
sycophantic than OLMo-2-32B (71.6%), 46.4 against 27.1. With the 7B model
chosen, the student reaches 17.9; with the 32B model chosen, 11.9, about the
SFT level. That pair follows sycophancy, not capability. Qwen3-4B is
slightly more sycophantic than Qwen3-14B (6.7 against 5.3). With 4B chosen the
student scores 6.6, and with 14B chosen 24.5. That pair follows capability.
Two pairs do not settle it, and the paper does not analyse either.

**The rise is confined to the prompts that name no alternative.** On the six
prompts that name a wrong option, OLMo-3-7B-SFT already flips 97.5% of the
time and DPO 96.7%, so the DPO effect is measurable only where the SFT model
is not at ceiling. In Tülu the two subsets move in opposite directions under
delta-learning DPO: the letter-naming rate falls from 71.4 to 59.7 while the
no-letter rate rises from 11.4 to 27.0. The authors say rank order is mostly
preserved and that their claims are relative (App. A.3). The finding's claims
hold for pushback that disputes without proposing an answer.

**The reversed arms are degraded models.** Students trained with Qwen3-0.6B
as the chosen teacher inherit its verbose style. On full MMLU the swapped arm
scores 39.3% with 21.33% unparseable answers, against 57.7% for the original
labels (Table 2). On the shared set they still answer correctly at turn 1,
so the 0.6% is measured. But the label reversal changes much more than
sycophancy, and the near-zero rate is not a clean opposite of the 38.1.

**Some objective arms are partly unparseable.** For APO-Down only 54.8% of
turn-1 answers reproduce and 48.85% of second turns have no parseable answer,
and IPO has 27.20% unparseable (Table 3). Rates are taken over rated
interactions. The paper does not say whether an unparseable second turn is
dropped or counted as a switch. Either way these two arms' rates are
confounded, and KTO (13.86%), APO-Zero (10.69%) and the original delta arm
(10.20%) are affected to a lesser degree.

**Diffuseness is inferred from failed searches.** The neutral-data claim rests on
a regex scan and a judge that is not validated against humans. The diffuseness
claim rests on two filtering methods finding nothing and on the dose curve.
This shows those methods cannot locate the signal. It does not show that no
subset carries it. The agreement judge measures stance endorsement in general
chat, not answer-switching under pushback, so a null gap on it does not rule
out a narrower cue.

**Breadth.** Two students, both 7–8B, one evaluation format, and one run per
condition. The Wilson intervals cover evaluation sampling, not training
variance, and the regression's points carry no training-seed spread. The
authors list the model range and the single setting as limits (§7).

**Not subliminal learning in the concept's sense.** The authors set the result
beside subliminal learning only as unintended generalization from benign data.
They note that the data was not controlled to remove semantic references the
way those settings are (§6). No teacher shares the student's base model.
The transferred quantity is the gap between two teachers, which contrastive
objectives amplify well beyond what SFT on the chosen responses carries.

## Concepts

- [Sycophancy](../concepts/sycophancy.md) — instantiation at the
  training-objective stratum, with an intervention design: the teacher pair is
  manipulated on fixed prompts, with label reversal and SFT-on-chosen as
  controls. It adds a route in which the preference data is not more agreeable
  and the behaviour tracks the teacher pair under contrastive objectives. The
  filtering result is diffuse-signal resistance.

## Cross-references

- [Subliminal learning](../concepts/subliminal-learning.md) — adjacent, not
  instantiated. Behaviour moves from teacher models to a student through data
  that does not show it, but without a shared base model, through unscrubbed
  chat data, and as a teacher-pair difference that contrastive objectives
  amplify.
  The primary [Cloud et al. finding](2025-subliminal-learning.md) is cited by
  the paper. The LLS method it tests comes from Aden-Ali et al.'s
  logit-linearity paper on subliminal effects (arXiv:2602.04863; not filed).
- [Emergent capabilities](../concepts/emergent-capabilities.md) — the paper
  frames the result as unintended generalization from benign data beside
  emergent misalignment. The sycophancy concept's adjacency note treats a
  known training origin as what separates sycophancy from emergent
  misalignment; this paper identifies and manipulates one, in one pipeline.
